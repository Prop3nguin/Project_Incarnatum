## Talurian Time
> On Taluria, There are some interesting date/time implications. 
> Their civil days are equal to the days on earth, but they get a transient period of ~1.875 hours between every day.
> This results in 15 extra hours per week, which it should be noted is where their light cycle lives.

Time Calculations :
| hours/day | hours/week | days/week | weeks/month | months/year |
|:---------:|:----------:|:---------:|:-----------:|:-----------:|
| 24        | ~207       | 8         | 9           | 4           |

### Talurian weeks
> Half of a Talurian week is considered "night" and the other "day" as we would describe them in english. 

Days of the week :
| 1st     | 2nd          | 3rd        | 4th           | 5th      | 6th           | 7th         | 8th            |
|:-------:|:------------:|:----------:|:-------------:|:--------:|:-------------:|:-----------:|:--------------:|
| New Tal | First Waxing | First Half | Second Waxing | Full Tal | First Waining | Second Half | Second Waining |
| Day     | 3/4 Light    | 1/2 Light  | 1/4 Light     | Night    | 1/4 Light     | 1/2 Light   | 3/4 Light      |
> Note :
> 
> The above table doesn't account for the moon (Tal's) light reflection, 
> which is ~30X brighter than earth's moon. this, means that the side of the planet that faces Tal 
> Never really experiences nightime. even on Full Tal, Someone could read a book outside in the moonlight.
